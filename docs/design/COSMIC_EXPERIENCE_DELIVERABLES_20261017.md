## 1. Core experience

MusicOnVerse must feel like entering a living, cinematic universe built for music creation. It must not feel like an ordinary desktop application with a dark background and space graphics. The user should feel launched into space, arrived in a vast cosmic environment, and traveling through it while creating, editing, mixing and mastering.

**The defining experience is EVER-DRIFTING THROUGH SPACE.** It must be immersive, cinematic, fluid, continuous and present throughout the application, not just the opening screen. The cosmic environment is the application's actual visual world, not wallpaper behind conventional panels.

## 2. Opening: launch → arrival → ever-drifting → orientation

1. **Launch:** rapid forward acceleration into space. Stars, distant celestial bodies, cosmic dust and depth move past the user to communicate acceleration, momentum and scale. This is travel into the universe, not a generic animation.
2. **Arrival:** forward motion stops suddenly and decisively, communicating a powerful arrival in vast space.
3. **Ever-drifting:** after the stop, settle into slow continuous drift. Distant stars, nebulae, planets and depth layers continue moving subtly, suggesting suspension and travel.
4. **Orientation:** main navigation appears naturally in that environment, inviting the user to choose a destination.

Acceleration, decisive stop and slow drift must be distinguishable and intentionally cinematic. Avoid generic loading screens, fade-to-black substitutes or static wallpaper with a few moving stars. Navigation must remain accessible; reduced motion must not remove destination access.

## 3. Two main destinations and naming

**A — former Workbench:** streamlined existing music workflow, reference-track functionality, one-click mastering/mix-fix and existing audio processing. This makes common shaping/repair/refinement approachable without requiring full DAW navigation.

**B — former3W:** full Logic Pro–inspired music-production environment: arrangement, timeline, tracks, audio editing, mixing, processing, plugins and the other established production capabilities. This DAW is current product scope, not a future-only requirement.

Workbench and3W are provisional, not final names. Develop them as a coherent pair: distinctive, atmospheric, natural and memorable for serious music software, not game levels or science-fiction novelty. Preserve functional clarity. Present a considered naming direction rather than finalizing arbitrarily; apply selected terminology consistently across opening/navigation/headings/components. Keep descriptive provisional labels while name selection is outstanding; do not block engineering or silently declare a pair approved.

## 4. One continuing cosmic environment everywhere

The cosmic experience must continue in both destinations. They are different working areas in the same universe, not separate applications or conventional interfaces.

Preserve continuous subtle space travel, convincing depth/distance, different apparent speeds for depth layers/celestial objects, atmospheric lighting/restrained illumination, smooth transitions and consistent identity. Movement remains present during intensive audio work. The application must feel like an active ever-drifting universe in which music production happens naturally.

## 5. Floating with the user — not glass panels over wallpaper

Interface, environment, lighting, depth and movement must be visually integrated. Working surfaces should feel embedded in, suspended within or naturally integrated into the environment, floating alongside the user.

Use lighting, edge illumination, shadows, atmospheric depth and environmental reflections where appropriate to establish that relationship. Keep scenery perceptible through and around working surfaces without sacrificing readability. Interface elements must feel spatially grounded, not unrelated rectangular overlays. Preserve openness, scale and continuous movement.

Avoid excessive glassmorphism, generic frosted cards, thick borders, stacks of floating rectangles, and dashboard layouts over a space image. Useful panels and precise controls remain necessary; the goal is environmental integration, not unreadable transparency. Earlier bounded opaque-workplane checks are not a mandate to keep the entire product as an opaque conventional dashboard.

## 6. Full DAW environment

Use Logic Pro as a reference for functional density, organization and depth, not a visual template. Support expected professional workflows: tracks, timeline navigation, audio editing, processing, plugin adjustment, mixing and mastering as functionality is implemented.

Keep arrangement/timeline accurately aligned; track controls/mixer channels hierarchical; plugin parameters legible/manipulable; waveforms/meters/values/edit boundaries precise; dense areas organized. Show the cosmic environment around and within surfaces wherever layout allows. Do not deliver a gray/black conventional DAW with decorative wallpaper. Do not sacrifice professional usability to spectacle or present unimplemented controls as working.

## 7. Sylune — one AI companion

**Sylune is the only AI companion.** No duplicate assistant persona, competing character or additional companion.

Sylune should accompany the user in both destinations: persistent and integrated, not merely a chatbot hidden behind a button, an oversized character panel, unrelated floating widget, customer-support chat or separate app over the DAW. Access must be consistent and non-obstructive, with a visual identity native to the same cosmic world.

Preserve the distinction between presentation and actual capability. Preserve existing real integrations where any exist. Do not invent AI operations, providers, thinking states, responses, suggestions or execution. A truthful unavailable/error/context-help state is required when capability is missing; communicate the actual integration gap to lead.

## 8. Motion, cinematography and atmosphere

Implement carefully animated cinematic scale, smooth continuous movement, credible depth, controlled acceleration/deceleration, persistent celestial motion, restrained glow/light, suspension/forward travel and fluid destination transitions.

Use multiple depth layers with different movement, not identical transforms everywhere. Motion must coexist with long music sessions but remain strong enough that space never feels static. Avoid excessive parallax, distraction, aggressive particles, camera shake or animation competing with production. Environment performance must not make audio, editing or controls sluggish.

## 9. Non-negotiable principles

1. Ever-drifting persists across opening and both destinations.
2. Opening communicates distinct launch, arrival and slow drift.
3. Interface belongs to the world, not wallpaper plus glass cards.
4. Both destinations retain distinct functional purposes.
5. One Sylune remains present throughout.
6. Precision/readability take priority when effects conflict with work.
7. Cosmic naming aids comprehension rather than obscuring it.
8. Cinematic, not gimmicky: no aliens, spaceships, gaming interfaces or unrelated sci-fi decoration.
9. Opening/navigation/workspaces/transitions/Sylune form one coherent universe.
10. Serious music-production software: atmosphere enhances work rather than replacing or weakening it.

## 10. Intended emotional result

Enter a vast living cosmos, feel launched into space, arrive and begin drifting. Choose a destination and keep drifting while creating. The interface feels suspended alongside the user and integrated into space. Sylune accompanies them. Reference work, mastering, timeline edits, plugin adjustment and complete-project mixing remain experiences inside that same universe.

**MusicOnVerse is not ordinary music software wearing a space-themed skin. It is a complete music-production environment conceived as a continuous journey through space.**

## 13. Linked Sessions — core, actual project-to-project updates

Deliver an interface for linking sessions and updating target material from source without requiring export, bounce, download or re-upload. This is a core workflow, not an optional visual feature.

Supported product workflows must include Mix→Master, Mix→Processing→Master, multiple source sessions into a target where supported, entire stereo project transfers, selected track/stem transfers, and updating linked material without replacing unrelated target work. Do not imply support beyond actual implemented service capabilities; missing backend work must be identified explicitly and coordinated with lead, not faked or dismissed as future-only scope.

###13.1 Discoverable actions and relationship information

Provide **Link Session** and **Update from Source** wherever relevant. Cosmic terminology may refine labels/placement only if source/target meaning remains immediately understandable.

Show source name, target name, link status, included tracks/stems/full-project scope, whether relevant source changed since successful update, whether update is available/in progress/completed/failed/review-required, and last successful update time or revision where available. Do not invent timestamps or revisions. Keep relationships understandable without clutter/interruption.

###13.2 Create/configure a link

User selects source session, selects/opens target session, chooses whole stereo project or supported tracks/stems, reviews the relationship, confirms it and sees it in the real session interface. Selective linking must show included and excluded source tracks. No manual bounce/upload requirement to establish the relationship.

###13.3 Update from Source

A real established link must refresh the intended target material after relevant source changes. Example: change SessionA's mix, open mastering SessionB, select Update from Source; identify relevant changes and update linked material without an export/reimport round trip.

Preserve target-owned processing, routing, plugins and independent tracks wherever the contract specifies them. An update does not authorize replacement of the whole target project. Unsupported ownership/processing must be diagnosed, not silently flattened or discarded.

###13.4 Selective track/stem links

Allow supported selected material to be linked and updated independently. Example: refresh only drums into a target without replacing bass/instruments/vocals/independent processing. Show link scope clearly. Manage multiple independent incoming links where supported. Excluded-track edits must not cause unintended linked replacement or target mutation. Actual bus stems and sums of selected tracks are distinct; do not label one as the other.

###13.5 Chained sessions

Represent and support controlled Mix→Processing→Master dependencies. Show pending/completed states per stage. Upstream change does not mean downstream has updated. Each stage follows its exact dependency/update rules. Avoid cycles and ambiguous chains. No silent automatic cascade or false currentness claim from upstream-only success.

###13.6 Review before apply

Show source/target, affected linked scope, exact source revision, relevant detected changes, target modifications/conflicts requiring a decision, and expected update scope. Provide apply/cancel/conflict-resolution actions where implemented. Distinguish proposal from committed update. If source changes during review, stale approval must not silently apply obsolete audio as current; invalidate/re-review according to actual service rules.

###13.7 Honest lifecycle and failure states

Cover link established, source unchanged, source changes detected, update available, preparing, awaiting review, applying, completed, failed, conflict, outcome uncertain, and recovery/reconciliation required, as applicable.

Do not claim interrupted updates succeeded without verification. If commit outcome is unknown, show uncertainty and the supported recovery/query path; do not encourage blind retries that may duplicate material or corrupt consistency. Preserve last valid target material on failure/cancellation/incompatibility.

###13.8 Link management

Inspect relationships and scope, navigate source/target, remove or reconfigure where supported. Removing a link is distinct from deleting audio/session content; communicate destructive consequences before action. Distinguish linked from independent material without overwhelming the DAW.

###13.9 Integrated cosmic UI

Link actions, relationship representation, status and review belong in the existing session/DAW interface with the same light/depth/motion language. Keep source→target direction clear and detailed reviews legible while ever-drifting continues. Sylune can help explain only where an actual integration supports it. No separate disconnected link utility or unrelated generic dialog design.

###13.10 Functional acceptance — verify against real service/project state

1. Establish a source→target link.
2. Create a whole-project link where supported.
3. Create a selected-track/stem link where supported.
4. Detect relevant source changes.
5. Apply intended changes to correct target material with Update from Source.
6. Preserve unrelated target work and target-owned processing.
7. Accurately represent chained pending/completed states.
8. Handle conflicts, failure and uncertain outcomes honestly.
9. Prevent stale review from silently applying an obsolete revision.
10. Remove relationships without inadvertently deleting content.
11. Remain usable at supported desktop sizes.
12. Exercise actual linked-session service and real project state, not simulated records.

If a required backend/service capability is absent, identify the exact limitation and send a concrete integration dependency to lead. No mock data, simulated success or controls that do nothing. Completion requires users to understand/create/inspect/review/execute whole-project or supported selected-material updates without a manual export/reimport workflow.
